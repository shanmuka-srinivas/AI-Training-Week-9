# Classic Hummus

## About this dish

Hummus lives or dies on two things, and neither of them is the tahini brand. The
first is cooking the chickpeas far past the point where you would call them done,
until the skins slip and a chickpea collapses under no real pressure at all. The
second is blending everything while the chickpeas are still hot.

The bicarbonate of soda in the cooking water raises the pH, which breaks the
pectin down faster and gets you to collapse-soft chickpeas in under an hour
rather than in three. It is the single largest lever in the recipe.

The third column is a percentage of the dry chickpea weight, in the same style as
the bread cards in this book.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Dried chickpeas, soaked overnight | 300 g | 100% |
| Light tahini | 240 g | 80% |
| Lemon juice | 90 g | 30% |
| Garlic, peeled | 15 g | 5% |
| Iced water | 120 g | 40% |
| Fine sea salt | 9 g | 3% |
| Ground cumin | 3 g | 1% |
| Total | 777 g | 259% |

## Method

Simmer the soaked chickpeas with a pinch of bicarbonate of soda until the skins slip
off and a chickpea collapses under light pressure, roughly fifty minutes.

Blend the hot chickpeas with the garlic and lemon juice, then add the tahini and
stream in the iced water until the puree turns pale and loose.

Season with salt and cumin, then let the hummus sit for thirty minutes so the garlic
mellows before serving.

## Storage and troubleshooting

Hummus keeps for four days in the fridge under a film of olive oil. It firms up
cold, so let it sit out for twenty minutes and loosen it with a spoonful of iced
water before serving.

Grainy hummus means the chickpeas were under-cooked. Bitter hummus means the
tahini was old, since tahini oxidises quickly once opened. A dull, pasty texture
means not enough iced water went in during blending.

## Allergen note

Contains sesame from the tahini. Free from gluten, dairy and egg.
