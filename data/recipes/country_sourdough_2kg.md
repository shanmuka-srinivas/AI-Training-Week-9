# Two-Kilo Country Sourdough

- recipe_id: R-101
- cuisine: french
- dietary_tags: vegan, dairy-free, contains-gluten
- yield: one 2 kg boule
- hydration: 75%

## About this loaf

This is the loaf the test kitchen bakes when it wants to know whether a new sack
of flour is any good. There is nowhere to hide in it. Two flours, water, a stiff
levain and salt, on a schedule slow enough that any weakness in the wheat shows
up as a slack, spreading dough on the bench rather than a domed one. A two
kilogram boule is also the largest single piece most domestic ovens will take,
which is why the formula is written at this size rather than scaled down from a
smaller test bake and then quietly rounded.

The third column of the table is baker's percentage, which means every
ingredient is written as a fraction of the total flour weight rather than of the
finished dough. Baker's percentage is why the white flour and the rye flour add
up to one hundred and everything else runs past it. If you want to make half a
loaf, halve the weights, not the percentages, and expect the crust to be thinner
because the surface area to mass ratio has moved.

Read the whole method before you begin. The timings here are not suggestions. A
coil fold taken twenty minutes late produces a different loaf, and the overnight
retard in the cold is what gives the crust its blisters rather than a merely
brown surface.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Strong white bread flour | 900 g | 90% |
| Wholemeal rye flour | 100 g | 10% |
| Water (28 C) | 750 g | 75% |
| Stiff wheat levain | 200 g | 20% |
| Fine sea salt | 20 g | 2% |
| Total dough | 1970 g | 197% |

## Method

Combine the two flours with the water and leave the mixture to autolyse for
forty-five minutes. Nothing else goes in at this stage, so the flour has time to
hydrate on its own and the gluten begins to organise without any interference.
The mixture will look rough and torn when you cover it and smooth and extensible
when you come back, and that change is the whole reason for the wait.

Add the levain and squeeze it through the dough until it disappears. The fine sea
salt goes in last, after the autolyse and after the levain is fully dispersed,
held back deliberately so it cannot tighten the dough before the flour has drunk
its water. See the ingredient table above for the exact weight. Salt added at the
start will slow the levain and stiffen the mass at exactly the point where you
want it slack and easy to work.

Bulk ferment for four hours at 25 C with a coil fold every forty-five minutes for
the first three hours, then leave the dough alone. Lift the dough from the middle
with both hands, let the ends fall under their own weight, and set it back down
turned ninety degrees. Four folds is usually enough. If the dough still tears
rather than stretches on the fourth fold, the flour is weaker than the formula
assumes and the bulk should be cut short by half an hour.

Preshape on an unfloured bench with a bench knife, dragging the mass towards you
so the surface tightens, and rest it for thirty minutes under a cloth. Shape into
a tight boule, seam side up in a floured banneton, and retard overnight at 4 C
for between twelve and sixteen hours.

Bake from cold in a covered Dutch oven at 250 C for twenty-five minutes, then
uncovered at 220 C for a further twenty-five minutes. Score once, deeply, at a
shallow angle. The loaf is done when the base sounds hollow and the internal
temperature has passed 96 C.

## Storage and troubleshooting

Cool the loaf on a wire rack for at least four hours before cutting. Cutting a
warm sourdough tears the crumb and drives off the steam that would otherwise
keep it soft for another day. Once cut, store it face down on a wooden board
rather than in plastic, which softens the crust within an hour.

A flat, spreading loaf almost always means the bulk ferment ran long rather than
the shaping was poor. A dense band along the base means the opposite: the dough
went into the oven under-fermented and the crumb collapsed under its own weight
before the crust set. A pale crust after the full bake time usually means the
oven never actually reached 250 C, whatever the dial claimed.

The loaf freezes well if you slice it first and wrap the slices in pairs. It
keeps for three months frozen and toasts straight from the freezer.

## Allergen note

Contains wheat and rye gluten. Free from dairy, egg, nuts, fish and shellfish.
