# Rosemary Focaccia

- recipe_id: R-001
- cuisine: italian
- dietary_tags: vegan, dairy-free, contains-gluten
- yield: one 30 x 40 cm tray
- hydration: 80%

## About this bake

Focaccia is the most forgiving bread in this book and the easiest to ruin
through timidity. The dough is slack enough that beginners panic and add flour,
and adding flour is exactly what turns a light, open focaccia into a flatbread.
Trust the wet dough. It will be sticky in the bowl and it will be fine.

The olive oil is doing three jobs at once: it enriches the crumb, it fries the
base against the hot tray, and the portion poured over the top before baking is
what makes the dimples crisp instead of dry. Use an oil you would happily put on
a salad, because you will taste it.

The third column is baker's percentage against the flour weight.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Strong white bread flour | 800 g | 100% |
| Water (24 C) | 640 g | 80% |
| Extra virgin olive oil | 64 g | 8% |
| Fresh yeast | 16 g | 2% |
| Fine sea salt | 16 g | 2% |
| Fresh rosemary, picked | 12 g | 1.5% |
| Total dough | 1548 g | 193.5% |

## Method

Whisk the fresh yeast into the water until it dissolves, then add the flour and mix
by hand until no dry patches remain. Rest the shaggy dough for twenty minutes, then
work in the salt and half of the olive oil, squeezing the dough between your fingers
until it turns smooth and slack.

Bulk ferment for three hours at room temperature, folding the dough over on itself
every forty-five minutes. Oil the tray generously, tip the dough in, and let it
relax for a further hour before dimpling it with wet fingertips.

Scatter the rosemary, pour over the remaining olive oil, and bake at 240 C for
twenty-two minutes until the top is blistered and deep gold.

## Storage and troubleshooting

Focaccia is at its best two hours out of the oven and is honestly past its best
the following day. Warm leftovers in a hot oven for four minutes rather than in
a microwave, which turns the crumb to rubber.

A dense, tight crumb means the dough was over-floured or under-proofed. Dimples
that vanish during baking mean the dough was pressed before it had relaxed. A
soggy base means the tray was not oiled generously enough or the oven was cold.

## Allergen note

Contains wheat gluten. Made in a kitchen that also handles sesame and tree nuts.
