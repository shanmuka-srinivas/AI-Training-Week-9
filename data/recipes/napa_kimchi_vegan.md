# Vegan Napa Cabbage Kimchi

- recipe_id: R-105
- cuisine: korean
- dietary_tags: vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, nut-free
- yield: 2.0 kg
- hydration: not applicable

## About this ferment

Anchovy fish sauce and salted shrimp do two jobs in a traditional kimchi. They
supply glutamates and nucleotides, which is the savoury depth people notice, and
they supply free amino acids that the lactobacilli feed on early in the ferment.
Removing them and putting nothing back gives you a kimchi that tastes thin and
that sours more slowly, which is why this is written as its own formula with its
own weights rather than as a note at the bottom of the traditional card.

The replacement is a kelp and dried shiitake stock. Kelp brings glutamate,
dried shiitake brings guanylate, and the two together are more savoury than
either on its own. The shiitake powder stirred in at the end is there for body
rather than for flavour, and it is what stops the paste tasting watery.

Because the stock is less salty than fish sauce, this formula ferments a day
longer at room temperature than the traditional one. The third column is a
percentage of the cabbage weight, as on the traditional card, so the two can be
read side by side.

## Method

Brine the quartered cabbage in the coarse sea salt for four hours exactly as you
would for the traditional version, salting the thick white ribs more heavily
than the leaf tips, turning once and rinsing three times. Drain upside down for
an hour.

Simmer a strip of dried kelp with dried shiitake for twenty minutes to build the
stock that stands in for fish sauce, then strain it and cool it completely. Do
not let the kelp boil hard or the stock turns slimy and bitter. The shiitake
powder is stirred in afterwards for the savoury depth the anchovy would
otherwise have carried, and it goes in cold so it does not clump.

Whisk the cooled stock into the rice porridge with the gochugaru, garlic and
ginger, fold through the radish and spring onion, and rub the paste into the
cabbage leaf by leaf. The paste is looser than the traditional one because the
stock is thinner than fish sauce, so pack the jars a little more firmly to
compensate.

Pack into jars and leave at 20 C for 3 days, which is a day longer than the fish
sauce version needs, then refrigerate at 4 C for two weeks. Taste on the third
day rather than trusting the clock: the ferment is ready to move to the cold
when the brine is cloudy and the cabbage has lost its raw crunch at the rib.

## Storage and troubleshooting

This keeps for four months in the fridge, which is shorter than the traditional
version, because the lower salt and the absence of fish sauce both let it
acidify faster. Taste it monthly rather than assuming it is holding steady.

Keep everything below the brine line and lift off any flat white yeast film. If
the paste separates and pools at the bottom of the jar, the stock was not cooled
before it went into the porridge.

A ferment that has gone soft rather than sour was drained badly after brining.
There is no rescue for it as kimchi, but it makes a decent base for a stew.

## Allergen note

Free from fish, shellfish, gluten, dairy, egg and nuts. Suitable for vegans.
