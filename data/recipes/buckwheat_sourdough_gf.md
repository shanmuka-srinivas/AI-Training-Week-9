# Gluten-Free Buckwheat Sourdough

- recipe_id: R-103
- cuisine: international
- dietary_tags: gluten-free, vegan, dairy-free, nut-free
- yield: one 1.4 kg pan loaf
- hydration: 110%

## About this loaf

Buckwheat is not wheat and is not related to it. It is a seed from the same
family as rhubarb, which is why a loaf built on it can be sold to somebody with
coeliac disease and why it tastes nothing like a wheat loaf. Expect something
dark, faintly grassy and close-crumbed, closer to a Baltic dark bread than to a
white sourdough.

Because there is no gluten at all, every scrap of structure in this loaf comes
from psyllium husk. Psyllium is a soluble fibre that grabs many times its own
weight in water and turns into a gel that behaves, mechanically, a little like a
gluten network. Take it out and you have a bowl of wet flour that bakes into
gravel. This is the one ingredient in the formula that cannot be substituted or
scaled by eye.

The hydration reads as one hundred and ten percent, which sounds absurd until
you remember that the psyllium is holding most of it and that buckwheat and
brown rice flour are both far thirstier than wheat. The third column is baker's
percentage against the combined flour weight.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Wholegrain buckwheat flour | 400 g | 66.7% |
| Brown rice flour | 200 g | 33.3% |
| Water (30 C) | 660 g | 110% |
| Buckwheat starter | 120 g | 20% |
| Psyllium husk, whole | 18 g | 3% |
| Fine sea salt | 12 g | 2% |
| Total dough | 1410 g | 235% |

## Method

Stir the psyllium husk into the water and leave it for ten minutes until it
thickens into a loose gel. This gel is the only structure the loaf will ever
have, so do not skip the wait and do not try to speed it up with hot water,
which makes the gel lumpy and uneven. Whole husk is specified rather than
powder; powder gels faster but sets much tighter and produces a rubbery crumb.

Fold the flours, the starter and the salt into the gel until the mixture is
uniform. It will look like thick porridge and that is correct. There is no
kneading stage, no folding schedule and no shaping. Working it harder achieves
nothing because there is no network to develop.

Transfer to a lined pan and level the top with a wet spatula. A parchment sling
makes the finished loaf far easier to lift out, since the crumb is fragile until
it is fully cold.

Proof for five hours at 26 C. The loaf will rise by about a third and no more.
Do not wait for it to double, because it never will, and a loaf left to try will
be sour and flat.

Bake at 220 C for sixty minutes. The crust darkens early, so cover the top
loosely with foil after the first thirty-five minutes if it is browning faster
than you would like.

## Storage and troubleshooting

Cool completely on a rack for at least four hours before cutting. This loaf is
even less forgiving than a rye about being cut warm, because the psyllium gel is
still firming as it cools and a warm cut turns the crumb sticky.

A gummy layer at the base almost always means the loaf was cut too early. A
crumbly loaf that will not hold a slice means either the psyllium was short or
it was powder rather than whole husk. A grey or greenish tinge to the crumb is
normal in wholegrain buckwheat and is not a fault.

Slice the whole loaf once it is cold and freeze what you will not eat in two
days. It stales quickly at room temperature and revives well from frozen.

## Allergen note

Free from gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, fish and shellfish. Milled on gluten-free
dedicated equipment.
