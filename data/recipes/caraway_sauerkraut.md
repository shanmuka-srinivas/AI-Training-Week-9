# Small-Batch Caraway Sauerkraut

- recipe_id: R-106
- cuisine: german
- dietary_tags: vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, nut-free
- yield: one 500 ml jar
- hydration: not applicable

## About this ferment

Sauerkraut is the shortest ingredient list in this chapter and the least
forgiving of a careless weighing. Cabbage and salt, and the salt has exactly one
job: to hold back the organisms that would rot the cabbage for the two or three
days it takes the lactobacilli to acidify the jar enough to defend itself. Too
little and the jar goes soft and smells of drains. Too much and the ferment
stalls and never sours properly.

The percentage in the third column is the salt as a fraction of the shredded
cabbage weight, and two percent is the number worth memorising. It is the figure
the whole craft turns on. Scale the cabbage to whatever your jar holds, apply
two percent, and the ferment will behave the same at half a kilogram as it does
at ten. Weigh the cabbage after shredding and after discarding the core, not
before.

No brine is added to this jar. Every drop of liquid the cabbage sits under comes
out of the cabbage itself, drawn out by the salt and released by the massaging.
That is why the massaging stage cannot be skipped.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Weight | % of Base |
|---|---|---|
| White cabbage, finely shredded | 350 g | 100% |
| Fine sea salt | 7 g | 2% |
| Caraway seed | 2 g | 0.6% |
| Juniper berries, cracked | 1 g | 0.3% |
| Total | 360 g | 102.9% |

## Method

Reserve two outer cabbage leaves whole before shredding the rest, and set them
aside. They become the cap that holds everything under the brine at the end.
Quarter the cabbage, cut out the dense core, and shred the leaves as finely as
you can manage. Finer shreds release liquid faster and pack tighter.

Toss the shreds with the fine sea salt in a wide bowl and leave them for twenty
minutes. The salt needs that time to start drawing water out through the cell
walls, and massaging before it has done so is just hard work for nothing.

Massage firmly for five minutes until enough brine pools in the bottom of the
bowl to cover the cabbage when pressed. Squeeze rather than stir. The cabbage
should reduce visibly in volume and go from crisp to limp. If after five minutes
there is still not enough liquid, leave it another twenty minutes and try again
rather than adding water.

Add the caraway and the cracked juniper, then pack the cabbage into the jar in
small handfuls, pressing each one down hard with a fist or a wooden tamper
before adding the next. Pour in every drop of brine left in the bowl. Lay the
reserved whole leaves on top as a cap and weight them with a small glass or a
fermentation weight so nothing floats above the brine line.

Ferment at 18 C for fourteen days, burping the jar daily for the first week.
Fermentation is vigorous for the first three or four days and then quietens. A
cooler room means a slower, cleaner and generally better ferment, so 18 C is
preferable to 22 C even though it takes longer.

## Storage and troubleshooting

Move the finished jar to the fridge, where it will keep for six months and keep
slowly souring. Use a clean fork every time, because a fork that has been in
your mouth introduces organisms the acid may not defeat.

Cabbage that pokes above the brine will discolour and go soft, which is why the
cap leaves matter. A flat white film on the surface is kahm yeast and is
harmless; skim it off. Anything fuzzy or coloured means the jar is finished and
should go out.

A jar that smells of sulphur in the first three days is normal and passes. A jar
that still smells of sulphur in the second week did not have enough salt.

## Allergen note

Free from gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, fish and shellfish. Suitable for vegans.
